# Citations and References

**Requires** — Established body flow, language and global link/counter policy,
plus the actual bibliography source and publication style evidence.

**Owns** — Citation rendering, bibliography placement and heading, entry field
semantics, ordering, typography, links, and page flow.

**Emits** — Standard citation and bibliography author routes whose extracted
text, punctuation, emphasis, numbering, and layout agree with the target.

**Recheck** — Citation clusters and locators, citation/reference links,
bibliography heading spacing, field order and punctuation, long entries,
column/page breaks, and final-page/footer relations.

Process Citations and References as separate direct children of body flow. Within
References, open Bibliography entries as its one direct child, then close the
parent against placement, heading, ordering, and page flow.

- **Citations [T]** — citation key/target semantics; numeric/author-year
  form; cluster ordering and ranges; prefix/suffix, locator, brackets,
  punctuation, spacing, links, and line breaking.
- **References [C,T]** — bibliography source and placement; heading and
  outline behavior; sorting, filtering, numbering/counter, columns and page
  flow.
  - **Bibliography entries [C,T]** — author/title/container/year and other
    field semantics; label/number format; punctuation and emphasis; hanging
    indent; entry spacing; URL/DOI/link treatment; wrapping, keep, and breaks.

Represent each bibliographic fact once in its canonical data field. Let the
CSL and template turn those facts into labels, localized prefixes, punctuation,
emphasis, range notation, the bibliography heading, typography, and placement.

Trace citation and bibliography author syntax to expected policy and observed
extracted/rendered output. Require semantic agreement for field order,
punctuation, emphasis, and localized prose before interpreting remaining raster differences.
